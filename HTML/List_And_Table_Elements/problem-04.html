Important Laptop Buying Factors

  • Processor: Determines how quickly the laptop can perform tasks.

  • RAM: More RAM allows smoother multitasking.

  • Storage: SSD storage provides fast startup and application loading.

  • Display: Consider screen size, resolution, panel quality, and refresh rate.

  • Battery: Important for students who need to use their laptop throughout the day.

  • Warranty: A longer warranty provides additional protection after purchase.

How to Choose Your Laptop

  1. Decide your budget before comparing products.

  2. Identify your primary purpose, such as study, coding, gaming, or design.
  3. Choose a suitable processor and at least 8 GB of RAM for general student use.

  4. Prefer an SSD for faster boot times and application performance.

  5. Check the display size, resolution, and refresh rate.

  6. Compare battery capacity and expected battery life.

    7. Check warranty coverage and after-sales service.
  7. Compare the final prices and choose the laptop that best matches your needs.

Technical Terms Explained

RAM
Random Access Memory temporarily stores data used by running programs. More RAM generally improves multitasking.

SSD
Solid State Drive is a fast storage device used to store the operating system, applications, and personal files.

CPU
Central Processing Unit is the main processor responsible for executing instructions and performing calculations.

GPU
Graphics Processing Unit handles graphics and is especially important for gaming, video editing, and 3D applications.

Refresh Rate
Refresh rate is the number of times a display refreshes its image per second, measured in Hertz (Hz).

Battery Capacity
Battery capacity indicates the amount of electrical energy a battery can store, commonly measured in watt-hours (Wh).
